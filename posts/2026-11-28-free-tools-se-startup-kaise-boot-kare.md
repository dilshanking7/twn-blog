# Free tools se startup kaise boot kare

Language: English | Date: 2026-11-28 | Provider: gemini

Most students in my college think you need lakhs of rupees in seed funding just to launch a web app. I used to think the same while sitting in my room in Dhanbad, watching founders on X talk about their funding rounds. The reality is different. You can build, deploy, and run a full startup stack up to your first thousand users without spending a single rupee.

Before writing a single line of code, you need to map out what you are building. I use Figma’s free tier for UI design and wireframing. It gives you three draft files, which is plenty for designing an entire web app. For writing product requirements, feature lists, and daily logs, Notion’s free personal workspace handles everything without hitting paywalls.

For the frontend, open-source tech makes expensive templates irrelevant. Next.js paired with Tailwind CSS lets you build fast, responsive interfaces. Component libraries like shadcn/ui give you accessible, production-ready UI components for free. You do not need to buy UI kits.

Backend infrastructure used to cost serious money. Now, tools like Supabase give you a free PostgreSQL database, user authentication, and file storage. Their free plan covers up to 500 MB of database storage and 50,000 monthly active users. If your app uses a NoSQL architecture, Firebase provides Firestore and Auth with generous daily free limits.

Hosting frontend code is effectively free now. Vercel deploys Next.js applications directly from your GitHub repo, automatically managing SSL certificates and global CDN delivery. Cloudflare Pages and Render offer similar setups with generous free tiers for static assets and backend services.

A custom domain email makes your cold emails look legitimate. Instead of paying for Google Workspace immediately, Zoho Mail offers a free tier for custom domains. You get up to five email accounts with 5 GB of storage per user, accessible via their web client.

Tracking user behavior is critical from day one. PostHog provides a free tier of 1 million events per month, which includes product analytics, session replays, and feature flags. Setting it up takes five minutes, and it shows you exactly where users drop off in your onboarding flow.

Distribution does not require paid ads. Posting your build updates on X, showcasing your product on Reddit communities like r/sideproject, and submitting to Product Hunt costs zero money. Technical write-ups on Dev.to or Hashnode pull in early adopters who actually give constructive feedback.

The bottleneck for building a software business is no longer server costs or expensive licenses. The tools available today let anyone with an internet connection ship a working product. Build your app on free tiers, get real users, and only upgrade your services when your product starts generating revenue.
